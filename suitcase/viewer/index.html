Suitcase Panel Joints

Three nail-free ways to fix each panel to the body, both as a suitcase lid and as a shelf leg, built on your Rhino model (ai_to_cad_suitcase_demo.3dm) in its own coordinates. Every option uses the T-slot already on the front and back rings, so one slot does both jobs.

What changes in your model, for all three. Your panel's T-tongue (10.5 mm head) is wider than the ring's slot chamber (10 mm), and the slot is closed at both ends, so the tongue cannot get in. The slot lips are also only 1 mm. Each option widens the chamber to 14 mm (3 mm lips, 3 mm walls left in the 20 mm bar) and replaces the continuous tongue. The drawn links and pins are replaced by a new link and a tool-free ball-lock pin at each corner.

Bringing an option into Rhino

  1. Delete the parts each option replaces: the front and back frame rings and the 8 panel T-tongues (layer frame track), everything on screws/nails (24 objects), and the four 2.4 mm hinge plates on foldable surface.
  2. File › Import suitcase/exports/STEP/<option>_shelf.step or _suitcase.step. It lands in place: same units (mm) and coordinates as your file. It holds the rebuilt rings and all new parts, named by corner (FL, FR, BL, BR).
  3. Or keep your own rings and panels and BooleanDifference the cut tools in <option>_cuts.step: cut_ring_slot from each ring, cut_panel from each panel's rail and top bar (they are placed for the panels as drawn).

Branch claude/keen-ride-4okiiq, folder suitcase/. The rebuilt rings follow your 20 × 20 mm bars; their corner rounding is approximate.
