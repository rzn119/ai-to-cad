Three ways to make the hinge hold an angle

Each option is built on your hinge as it is in the .3dm: the same box, knuckle, arm and end piece, and the same pivot axis. Pick an option and move the arm. Turn on "Cut in half" to see the parts inside the channel.

A · no hardware

Click detent

13 V-notches on the back of the knuckle, one every 15°. A V-nub on a thin bridge in the channel floor clicks into them. Push the arm and it clicks to the next stop.

Stops
0, 15, 30 … 180°
Spring
bridge 6 × 1.0 × 24.5 mm, fixed at both ends, gives 0.57 mm
Hold
light: a panel's own weight, not a load
Print
in place, as yours
B · no hardware

Ratchet

Saw-tooth notches every 30° and a pawl in the floor. Lifting the arm ramps the pawl down and it clicks up stop by stop. Pushing the arm down meets a square face and is blocked. To lower it, press the pawl's front end down.

Stops
0, 30, 60 … 180°
Spring
pawl 6 × 1.2 mm, 25 mm cantilever, gives 1.0 mm
Hold
positive, against loads pushing the arm down
Print
in place; small support under the pawl
C · M4 bolt + printed knob

Clamp knob

The cone pivots become an M4 bolt through the walls and knuckle. Slits turn the walls beside the knuckle into flexible ears. Tighten the knob and the ears clamp the knuckle at any angle.

Stops
any angle
Clamp
ears close the 0.19 mm gaps each side
Hold
strongest, about 1 N·m when hand-tight (estimate)
Print
3 separate parts, no supports in the box
